# Example: Making Team Documents Concise

**Scenario**: Process-improvement topic — the user's wordy team documents need a way to become concise without losing meaning. Runs through all 8 phases on a non-product topic.

**Applies**: **facilitate-session**
**Phases**: **clarify-idea**, **identify-goals**, **explore-challenges**, **generate-ideas**, **evaluate-ideas**, **challenge-ideas**, **refine-idea**, **compile-actionable-plan**

## Input / Context
User request: "My team's documents — ADRs, solution docs, PR descriptions — are long and wordy. I want to make them concise, not wordy. Not sure where to start."

## Session Walkthrough

### 1. clarify-idea
Facilitator: "What does making documents concise mean to you?"
User: "Say more with fewer words — cut the fluff without losing meaning."
Facilitator: "Who is this for, and what problem does it solve?"
User: "Our engineering team. Wordy docs get skimmed or skipped, so decisions get lost."
Facilitator: "Are you starting fresh, or is there existing work?"
User: "Existing docs — they are written but bloated."
Facilitator: "Any assumption to test — for example, that concise means shorter?"
User: "I assumed shorter, but really every sentence should earn its place."

**Confirmed idea**: A way to make team documents concise — every sentence earns its place while the meaning stays intact.

### 2. identify-goals
Facilitator: "What do you want to achieve — for example, shorter docs, or faster reviews?"
User: "Both. Shorter docs and reviews that actually get read."
Facilitator: "How will you know it succeeded?"
User: "Documents drop by about 40% in length, and reviewers stop skimming."
Facilitator: "Which objective matters most?"
User: "Faster, complete reviews — concise is a means to that end."

**Confirmed goals**: cut document length ~40%; complete reviews, no skimming; a sustainable writing habit.

### 3. explore-challenges
Facilitator: "What challenges do you foresee — for example, writer habits or time pressure?"
User: "Writers are used to long-form, and there is constant time pressure."
Facilitator: "What could cause this to fail?"
User: "If it feels like extra work, nobody adopts it."
Facilitator: "Any constraints, such as tooling or budget?"
User: "No budget for tools — it has to work with a wiki and a repo."

**Confirmed challenges**: long-form writer habit; adoption effort; fear of losing nuance; no tooling budget.

### 4. generate-ideas
Facilitator: "Here are 5–7 diverse ideas, generated by analogy, inversion, combination, and elimination, presented as a mind map:"

```
Concise Documents
├── Structure
│   ├── BLUF: conclusion first
│   ├── One-line takeaway per heading
│   └── Tables over prose
├── Sentence rules
│   ├── 20-word sentence cap
│   ├── One claim per bullet
│   └── Banned-phrase list
├── Editing process
│   ├── "So what?" pass
│   ├── Sentence surgery
│   └── Before/after pairs
└── Feedback loop
    ├── Word-count target
    ├── Concise-writing checklist
    └── Review of the reviews
```

User: "BLUF with takeaway lines, one claim per bullet, and the 'so what?' pass stand out. Sentence surgery feels too fiddly to start."

**Confirmed candidates**: BLUF structure, one-line takeaways, one-claim bullets, banned-phrase list, "so what?" pass, concise-writing checklist, before/after examples.

### 5. evaluate-ideas
Facilitator: "Scoring the main candidates:"

| Idea | Value | Feasibility | Differentiation | Scope | Impact |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| BLUF + takeaway lines | High | High | High | Fits | High |
| One claim per bullet | High | High | Medium | Fits | High |
| "So what?" pass | High | Medium | High | Fits | High |
| Concise-writing checklist | High | Medium | Low | Fits | Medium |
| Banned-phrase list | Medium | High | Medium | Fits | Medium |
| Before/after examples | Medium | Medium | Medium | Fits | Medium |

Facilitator: "By effort vs impact, BLUF, one-claim bullets, and the 'so what?' pass win; the checklist supports them."

**Confirmed priorities**: BLUF structure, one-claim bullets, "so what?" pass, concise-writing checklist.

### 6. challenge-ideas
Facilitator: "Have you considered that hard caps might strip necessary nuance from technical decisions?"
User: "True — treat caps as ceilings, not targets; meaning comes first."
Facilitator: "What if the 'so what?' pass becomes an extra step people skip under time pressure?"
User: "Fold it into the checklist so it is a habit, not a gate."
Facilitator: "Is the checklist essential or a nice extra?"
User: "Essential — it is what makes the habit repeatable."

**Stress-tested ideas**: caps are ceilings, meaning first; "so what?" pass folded into the checklist; checklist is core.

### 7. refine-idea
Facilitator: "What is essential versus nice-to-have?"
User: "Essential: BLUF, one-line takeaways, one-claim bullets, and the checklist with the 'so what?' test. Nice-to-have: banned-phrase list, before/after examples."
Facilitator: "What would make it more robust?"
User: "A one-page style guide with worked examples."

**Refined idea**: A concise-writing workflow for team documents built on BLUF structure, one-claim bullets, and a checklist whose core step is the "so what?" test, backed by a one-page style guide.

### 8. compile-actionable-plan
The facilitator compiles the plan per **plan-output-format**:

## Refined Idea
A concise-writing workflow for team documents built on BLUF structure, one-claim bullets, and a checklist whose core step is the "so what?" test, backed by a one-page style guide.

## Goals
- Cut document length by ~40%
- Speed up reviews — reviewers read, not skim
- Make concise writing a sustainable habit

## Challenges & Solutions
| Challenge | Solution |
|---|---|
| Long-form writer habit | One-page style guide + checklist |
| Adoption effort under time pressure | Checklist folded into review, not a new gate |
| Fear of losing nuance | Caps are ceilings; meaning comes first |
| No tooling budget | Manual checklist + review pairs, no tools |

## Key Components
- BLUF headings with one-line takeaways
- One-claim bullets
- Concise-writing checklist with the "so what?" test
- One-page style guide with examples

## Next Steps
- Write the one-page style guide with BLUF and bullet rules
- Draft the checklist with the "so what?" test
- Rewrite one existing doc as a before/after example
- Pilot on two new docs and measure length
- Review adoption and adjust the checklist

Facilitator: "Here is the plan. Would you like to refine any section before we finalize?"
